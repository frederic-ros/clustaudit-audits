Interventional auditing of clustering algorithms

Not a ranking.
An audit.

A ranking says which method scored best on a dataset. An audit says under which conditions a method fails, at what level, and in what form: silently, or visibly enough for a user to notice.

Audit a new algorithm in three steps

1 · Write one functioncluster(X, seed) → labels

About ten lines of Python around your method. Label −1 marks a point your method leaves unassigned.

2 · Quick test in your browser

Your code runs locally, on three small generated datasets. You see the clusters it finds and whether it passes the pre-audit. Nothing is uploaded.

3 · Full audit, published

The fixed battery of the paper: the 75-dataset suite and six single-factor sweeps, 250 datasets (see them). You receive an audit card to cite and a badge for your README.

Reference audits

Nine classical methods under the paper's battery, protocol F. Each card gives a note per factor, where the method breaks, and how.

Submitted audits

"Breaks at": first level where the mean inlier ARI over five seeds falls below 0.5. "Fails silently": a well-formed but wrong partition. "Fails detectably": a degenerate output a user can see without labels (fewer than two clusters, or most points rejected).
